The Rise of Remote Online Notarization in Real Estate

Maria Gonzalez • September 15, 2026 • 8 min read

The real estate industry is experiencing a dramatic shift in how documents are notarized. Remote Online Notarization (RON) has moved from a niche technology to a mainstream solution that's reshaping how title companies, lenders, and signing agents conduct closings.

What Is Remote Online Notarization?

Remote Online Notarization allows a notary public to notarize documents for a signer who is not physically present. Instead, the notary and signer connect through a secure audio-video platform, using identity verification technology and digital signatures to complete the process.

Unlike traditional in-person notarization, RON enables closings to happen from anywhere — the signer's living room, a hospital bed, or even from overseas. This flexibility has made it an increasingly popular option for real estate transactions.

"RON isn't replacing traditional mobile notary services — it's expanding the options available to title companies and their clients. The best agencies offer both."

The State of RON Adoption

As of 2026, 47 states have enacted permanent RON legislation, up from just 23 in 2020. The pandemic accelerated adoption dramatically, and many states that implemented temporary RON measures have since made them permanent.

Key statistics on RON adoption:

  • 47 states with permanent RON laws
  • 68% of title companies now offer RON as an option
  • RON transactions grew 340% between 2020 and 2025
  • Average RON session takes 35 minutes (vs. 45 for in-person)

Benefits for Title Companies

For title companies considering RON integration, the benefits extend beyond simple convenience. RON platforms offer enhanced security features including knowledge-based authentication, credential analysis, and complete session recording for audit trails.

Perhaps most importantly, RON eliminates geographic constraints. A title company in New York can close a transaction for a signer temporarily located in Florida without the complexity of finding a local notary who meets their quality standards.

The Hybrid Approach

At SignSure, we believe the future isn't purely digital or purely in-person — it's hybrid. Many signers still prefer the personal touch of an in-person notary, especially for complex transactions like first-time home purchases. Others need the flexibility that RON provides.

The most successful title companies are those offering both options and letting the borrower choose. This approach maximizes convenience while maintaining the personal service that builds trust in real estate transactions.

Looking Ahead

As RON technology matures and more states adopt legislation, we expect to see further innovation in the space — from AI-assisted document review to blockchain-based notarization records. The companies that adapt to these changes while maintaining their commitment to accuracy and compliance will lead the industry forward.
